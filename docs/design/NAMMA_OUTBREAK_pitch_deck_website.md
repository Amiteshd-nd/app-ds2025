## Slide 1 — Title
**NAMMA OUTBREAK** — *You come to Bangalore to make a life. Then you have to survive it.*
A 32-bit, Zelter-angle pixel-art survival RPG set in a real, lived-in Bangalore. PWA — plays in any browser, installs like an app.

## Slide 2 — The hook
The only zombie game where you first have to find a PG, negotiate rent, miss the bus, and fall in love — before the city turns.

## Slide 3 — Why it's different
- Real Bangalore, not generic India: PGs, Darshinis, BMTC, Namma Metro, monsoon waterlogging, Kannada boards.
- Slow burn: a life-sim that *earns* its horror (Severance × World War Z × The Girl With All the Gifts).
- Light combat: tension and escape, not a shooter. Run, hide, reroute.
- Fail-forward: like Google Maps, a wrong turn reroutes — never a dead end.

## Slide 4 — The X-factor: a living city ("City Pulse")
- Moment marketing: real Bangalore events (festival, protest, water crisis) appear in-game for that week — shape only, names blurred. Instant social buzz.
- Not forced, discovery-based: events hide in corners; explorers are rewarded. Fuels curiosity + word of mouth.
- Even in the apocalypse, life goes on: a shopkeeper still runs an offer; a robbery still happens. Darkly real.

## Slide 5 — Business model (respectful, non-exploitative)
- In-game sponsors: cosmetic banners placed by real player-path heatmaps. Labelled, no pay-to-win, decay in Act 4.
- Game sales: free demo (Acts 0–1) → paid full game. No manipulative timers, no loot boxes.
- Seasonal live-ops: festival weeks, limited City Moments.
- Guard-rail: design for fun first; monetization never touches balance.

## Slide 6 — Engagement engine
- Core loop (Challenge→Action→Feedback→Progress→Reward→Curiosity) + felt pacing (Calm→Curious→Engaged→Tense→Reward).
- A new mechanic every 20–30 min; rewards every 5–10 min early.
- Three reward types — cosmetic, functional, progression — every act gives all three.

## Slide 7 — Emotional spine
Two small-town kids, one city. The player builds a life; an exhausted intern makes one tired mistake. Months later they share a hospital ward. Identity-fade mechanic: the city makes you forget yourself — the apocalypse gives it back.

## Slide 8 — Scale plan
- Phase 0: minimal playable loop (M0) — airport → chai → bus.
- Phase 1: vertical slice (Acts 0–1), playtest with 5–8 people who moved to Bangalore.
- Phase 2: Acts 0–2 + City Pulse + first sponsor pilot → free demo launch.
- Phase 3: full story, endings, live-ops. More cities later (same engine, new district data).

## Slide 9 — Why it grows
Shareable "My Bangalore" cards (days survived, ₹ spent on chai) → organic reach. City Pulse → recurring press moments. PWA → zero install friction, one link. Data-driven engine → new content is JSON, not code.

## Slide 10 — Tech & cost
Phaser + TypeScript PWA · data-driven (places/quests/NPCs = JSON) · AI-assisted art locked to one style reference · ~70-asset vertical slice under $100 · solo/small team, agile face-by-face build.
